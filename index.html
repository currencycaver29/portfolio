§ 2Quantitative Strategy Research

The following case studies were developed as part of Finance & Economics Club research initiatives and independent study. Each represents an end-to-end research process: hypothesis formation, data sourcing, statistical testing, strategy design, and out-of-sample evaluation.

2.1   Cointegration-Based Pairs Trading on NSE Large-Caps
2025
Finance & Economics Club, IIT Guwahati · Quantitative Strategies Cell
Statistical Arbitrage Cointegration Kalman Filter NSE Data Python

Motivated by the observation that structurally similar firms — same sector, correlated revenue streams, shared macro exposure — often exhibit long-run price equilibria despite short-run divergences, this study constructed a systematic pairs trading framework on NSE large-cap equities. The core hypothesis: when the spread between a cointegrated pair deviates sufficiently from its historical mean, a mean-reversion trade offers positive expected return with bounded risk.

Methodology

Cointegration was tested via Engle-Granger two-step procedure and Johansen trace test, with ADF confirmation on regression residuals. Hedge ratios were estimated dynamically using a Kalman filter state-space model — avoiding the stale fixed-ratio assumption of OLS. Mean-reversion speed was quantified by fitting an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process to the spread; half-life estimates drove holding period calibration. Entry and exit signals were generated on rolling z-score thresholds (±1.5σ entry, ±0.25σ exit). Position sizing followed Kelly criterion with a conservative fractional multiplier to account for estimation error. Walk-forward cross-validation was applied across three out-of-sample windows to prevent in-sample overfitting — a methodological discipline often absent in undergraduate quant work.

Sector Coverage

Banking (HDFCBANK/ICICIBANK, AXISBANK/KOTAKBANK), Pharma (SUNPHARMA/DRREDDY), IT Services (INFY/TCS, WIPRO/HCLTECH), FMCG (HINDUNILVR/NESTLEIND). Thirteen pairs tested; seven passed cointegration screens at 5% significance.

Results: Walk-forward Sharpe ratio 1.84 on out-of-sample windows. Maximum drawdown 12.3%. Mean half-life 13.6 trading days. Strongest pair stability observed in banking sector; FMCG pairs exhibited highest half-life variance. Spread stationarity confirmed via ADF at 5% for all traded pairs.
2.2   Cross-Sectional Momentum and Factor Premia on BSE 500
2024–25
Finance & Economics Club, IIT Guwahati · Research Report
Factor Investing Fama-French Momentum IC Evaluation Fama-MacBeth Regression

This study examined whether standard factor premia documented in US markets replicate on the BSE 500 universe, and whether additional factors — specific to Indian market microstructure — offer incremental alpha. The practical motivation was to inform a systematic long-short factor portfolio suitable for the club's paper trading competition.

Factor Library Constructed

Momentum (1-month, 3-month, 12-month lookbacks with 1-month skip), short-term reversal (5-day), volume-adjusted return, earnings surprise drift (post-announcement drift), size (log market cap), and value (book-to-market). Each factor was evaluated on monthly Information Coefficient (IC), rank IC stability, autocorrelation, and turnover — assessing practical deployability alongside predictive signal.

Statistical Framework

Factor premia were estimated via Fama-MacBeth cross-sectional regression: independent OLS regressions run each month, time-series mean of slope coefficients taken as the premium estimate, with Newey-West standard errors correcting for residual autocorrelation across periods. Sector and market-beta neutralization applied before return attribution to isolate net factor performance.

Results: Mean monthly IC of 0.058 across combined factor model. 12-month momentum showed strongest and most persistent IC (0.071, t-stat 3.2). Short-term reversal decayed sharply beyond 5 trading days. Post-earnings drift significant in mid-cap segment; negligible in large-caps, consistent with faster price discovery in liquid names. Combined long-short decile spread: annualised 18.4% on risk-adjusted basis over study period.
2.3   Volatility Regime Detection and Options Strategy Adaptation
2025
Finance & Economics Club, IIT Guwahati · Independent Study
Volatility Modelling GARCH Hidden Markov Model Options India VIX

India VIX exhibits persistent regime behaviour — extended periods of suppressed volatility interrupted by sharp spikes around macro events (RBI policy, election cycles, global risk-off episodes). This study modelled NIFTY 50 volatility regimes to identify when short-volatility strategies (cash-secured puts, iron condors) offered favourable risk-reward versus when long-volatility positions (straddles, ratio spreads) were warranted.

A two-state Hidden Markov Model was fitted to realised NIFTY volatility (22-day rolling), separating low-volatility and high-volatility regimes. GARCH(1,1) was used within each regime to model conditional variance dynamics. Regime transition probabilities were used to construct a probabilistic signal for options strategy selection. Backtest covered 2019–2024, spanning COVID shock, 2022 inflation episode, and 2024 election volatility.

Results: HMM correctly identified 78% of regime transitions with a mean lead of 4.2 trading days. Short-vol strategy in low-regime months produced Sharpe of 1.41; long-vol strategy in high-regime months reduced drawdown by 34% versus unconditional positioning. Combined adaptive strategy outperformed static straddle approach by 620 bps annualised over backtest period.

3.1   Long Pitch: Dixon Technologies (NSE: DIXON)
2024
Sector: Electronics Manufacturing Services · Club Internal Competition
DCF Valuation EMS Sector PLI Scheme Comps Analysis

Thesis: Dixon Technologies is a structural beneficiary of India's Production-Linked Incentive scheme across consumer electronics, lighting, and mobile phones — with a management track record of winning new PLI contracts and an asset-light contract manufacturing model that compounds return on capital as revenue scales. At time of analysis, the market was pricing Dixon on near-term margin compression from customer concentration risk, creating a mispricing opportunity in the 3–5 year horizon.

Valuation

DCF across three scenarios (bear/base/bull) with WACC 11.2%, terminal growth 5.5%. Sensitivity analysis on PLI disbursement timelines and customer concentration (Apple, Samsung revenue share). Peer comps: Foxconn India, Amber Enterprises, Kaynes Technology. EV/EBITDA relative to EMS peers in Taiwan and Vietnam for global context.

Conclusion: Base-case intrinsic value implied 28% upside from pitch price. Key risk: single-customer revenue concentration above 40% threshold. Catalyst identified: new PLI tranche announcement and export ramp-up to Middle East markets. Pitch awarded best fundamental research in club internal competition.
3.2   Short Pitch: Overvalued Mid-Cap IT Services — Sector Thesis
2024
Sector: IT Services (Mid-Cap) · Club Research Cell
Short Thesis Revenue Quality Margin Analysis IT Services

Following the post-COVID IT services re-rating, a cohort of mid-cap IT firms traded at premium multiples (35–45x PE) that assumed sustained double-digit revenue growth — despite structural headwinds: BFSI client budget freezes, US discretionary spending slowdown, and rupee appreciation compressing export margins. This pitch argued for mean-reversion in multiples as growth disappointed consensus estimates.

Analysis decomposed revenue by geography and vertical, flagging over-exposure to discretionary digital transformation spending in US financial services — the segment most directly impacted by Fed tightening. Margin bridge analysis showed operating leverage assumptions embedded in consensus were inconsistent with historical hiring costs. Quantitative screen identified names trading above 2.5 standard deviations from 5-year forward PE mean.

Conclusion: Sector-wide multiple compression of 20–30% projected over 12–18 months as FY25 guidance disappointed. Identified three names as most vulnerable on revenue quality metrics. Thesis substantially realised within 9 months of publication.
§ 4Macroeconomic Case Studies

Macro analysis is the systematic study of the forces that move entire asset classes. The following case studies were developed as club research papers and competition submissions, with explicit linkage from macro thesis to market positioning.

4.1   RBI Monetary Policy Transmission and Indian Rate Markets
2024
Finance & Economics Club · Macro Research Paper
Monetary Policy RBI Rate Markets Yield Curve India Macro

This paper examined the effectiveness of RBI repo rate changes in transmitting to bank lending rates, G-sec yields, and ultimately inflation — with particular attention to the 2022–24 tightening cycle. The central question: does Indian monetary policy transmission operate symmetrically, or is the pass-through to credit markets structurally incomplete?

Analysis used VAR (Vector Autoregression) models on monthly data (repo rate, 10Y G-sec, MCLR, CPI, IIP output) to estimate impulse response functions. Key finding: repo rate shocks transmitted to 10Y yields within 2–3 months, but MCLR transmission lagged by 5–7 months due to excess liquidity buffer in the banking system. This lag created a window where bond markets priced in tightening ahead of actual credit tightening — a tradeable dynamic in rate markets.

Conclusion: Pass-through coefficient to MCLR estimated at 0.62 over 6-month horizon. G-sec curve steepening observed 45–60 days prior to MCLR repricing. Market implication: duration positioning in short-end bonds outperformed at cycle turns. Paper submitted to club journal; selected for inter-IIT finance symposium presentation.
4.2   INR/USD Dynamics — Carry, Current Account, and Capital Flows
2025
Finance & Economics Club · Currency Research Note
FX Carry Trade Current Account EM Currency Regression Analysis

The INR/USD rate is among the most politically sensitive and analytically complex exchange rates in emerging markets — simultaneously influenced by the RBI's managed float interventions, India's structural current account deficit, FII equity and debt flows, and global risk appetite. This note built a multi-factor model to explain INR depreciation pressure and identify when carry returns were likely to be overwhelmed by spot depreciation.

Explanatory variables: Brent crude (India imports ~85% of oil needs), US DXY index, FII net flows (equity + debt), current account balance as % GDP, and India-US 10Y rate differential. OLS regression with HAC standard errors across 2015–2024 monthly data. Carry trade return decomposed into interest differential earned versus spot loss realised.

Conclusion: Crude price and DXY together explain 61% of INR variance in the model. Rate differential contributes positively to carry but is offset during global risk-off episodes by FII outflows. Practical implication: INR carry trades require crude and DXY hedging overlays to be viable in risk-adjusted terms.
4.3   Indian Inflation Structure — Food, Fuel, and Core Dynamics
2024
Finance & Economics Club · Economic Analysis
Inflation CPI Decomposition Food & Fuel Policy Analysis

Indian CPI's unusually high weight in food (45.86%) makes headline inflation extremely sensitive to agricultural supply shocks — monsoon deficits, vegetable price spikes — that are structurally outside the RBI's influence. This creates a persistent tension: the RBI tightens in response to headline CPI, but the causal factor is supply-side, not demand-driven. This case study decomposed CPI into food, fuel, and core components to assess whether policy tightening in 2023–24 was targeting a controllable signal.

Conclusion: Core CPI (ex-food, fuel) trended down through 2023–24, reaching 3.8% — below the RBI's 4% target. Headline CPI remained elevated due to persistent vegetable price shocks (tomato, onion). Analysis suggested RBI tightening was responding to a noisy signal; rate cuts were justified by core dynamics 6 months before they arrived. Paper cited in club's inter-IIT macro debate submission.
